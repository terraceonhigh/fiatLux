The coffee pot was once complained about, the way it turned water acrid and then made the cup noodle gross. Even now, that bitter threatened to pollute the acidic notes of the rose hips and blackberry by whoever plucked today's blend. Though, a gentle overthrowing of this herbal vinegar would not be entirely undesirable.

The compressor kicked in and replaced the slight tinnitus in Oyá's ears. The LEDs on the board glowed while her professor sat in the next chair and read something that was definitely not from the university library.

*At least I am not out there slowly getting carbon monoxide poisoning like the sparkies.*

*Oh, blink and it'll be your turn digging pipes and laying wells out in the sticks, just try and enjoy it.*

*I am trying, get me that flask.*

She held out her mug.

The professor said something about oak chips and about the chemistry department.

---

The odometer slaved to a bob in the south tank stayed the same number while the day got warm. The professor folded a page out from the extracurricular publication.

A yellow LED came on.

*What is it, Oyá?*

*South tank, draining.*

*Wait, what time is it?*

The wall clock said 1000, Oyá's wristwatch said two-past-ten.

*Either way, that isn't right. What does sensor 342 say?*

*Gauge says dropping pressure, is that not one of the inbound pipes from the—*

*Yes.*

The landline phone did not have a number pad on the front. The operator got the professor the engineering office.

Before she connected them, though, the professor gave the handset to Oyá.

*You know enough.* Then it was a handshake.

The phone fizzed to life.

*South Station, Grand Central. Grand Central requests two trucks out to radio readings on gauge three-hundred-and-ten through to twenty, and three-hundred-and-forty through fifty.*

*And one more with a high-gain radio, I want the city on the line.* The professor shouted from the record room.

*And one communication truck to establish contact with city water works.*

*South Station receives.*

---

The map on the table was printed the same day Oyá was born. The professor's scrawl on it had yet to dry. The handset was on speaker.

*South Station, readback on gauge three-four-three.*

*Right, uh, sorry, field team one reported 250 kPa half an hour ago.*

*Radio them for another readout.*

*Standby...*

*243 kPa, Grand Central.*

*Thank you, South Station.*
