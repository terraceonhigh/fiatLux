The last doctor had left a note on the table, something about how Rusina is better at soups and Seia is better at pastries, as well as which family hearths would not pass health inspections if med students conducted health inspections. The note itself was held down by a grip-trainer, someone had carved *moo* on the handle.

Egghead flipped the note, and there was a small prayer to whoever is lactose intolerant.

The sign on her office said The Doctor is Out, find ~~him~~ her at this location.

The last doctor introduced her to the frequent fliers, and told her where to fire up the autoclave.

Hammers echoed down the street, someone was using a saw.

She said her greetings to Rusina, and Rusina had her kneel and learn how to squeeze an udder. The hammers gave a percussive rhythm as her arms wore out. The cow's name was Ginger.

The trucks left with a foot of bandage and a square of gauze wrapped around a hand. It was her opinion that some people should not be trusted with hammers.

*See you in the spring.*

---

Day broke, and she flipped the sign on her office to The Doctor is In. There sat a young woman in flannel on the porch, with a yard of sausage draped across her torso and a bundle of pelt at her feet.

*Doctor Egghead, right?*

*Actually, I prefer Rosa.*

*Egghead suits you more to me. Name's Diana, see that your office just opened, Doctor Rosa?*

*Just Rosa is alright, no Doctor.*

*Out here, we call things by their names, Doctor Rosa.*

*I accept the compliment. So, how may I be of service?*

*The old stump is sore again. The last doctor left you anything about that?*

*Hmm, I do not recall seeing it last night, if you can tell me about it? And please do come in.*

The pelts were set behind the door of the examination room.

Diana took off the sausage, and held it out.

*'sfor you, and the visit.*

*I couldn't possibly.*

*Yes you can. It's deer.*

*Well, in that case. Thank you, Diana. If you could take off your pants and take a seat on this bed?*

*'course.*

Rosa turned to face the wall while she did so. Diana rolled her eyes.

*Thank you, it is the left leg, correct?*

*Yes ma'am.*

Rosa gently leaned the prosthesis on the bed.

*I see that you have doubled up your socks.* The inside one was greasy.

*Had to since fall, the socket gets loose.*

*Every year?*

*Every year.*

*I am so glad to hear that you are managing that.*

*We make do.*

*And so, when and where does it hurt?*

*About afternoon and evening, after I am done walking the traps.*

*You're a trapper?*

*Yes ma'am.*

*I can already tell I will love the sausage. Now, does it hurt if I press... here?*

*Can't say so.*

*Here?*

*A bit sore.*

*What about here?*

*Ow, fuck—pardon me.*

*It's quite alright, so it hurts there after the day?*

*Yes ma'am.*

*Besides the sock, are you doing anything else about it?*

*Well, I try and stay in the blind more, get me more sit-down time in the day...*

*Okay, okay, that is very good.*

*And there is this lotion I been using. My boyfriend got it for me.*

*Hmm, do you have it on you?*

*Yes, should be in my satchel if you'll gimme a moment.*

*Of course.*

Some metallic things clinked together as she dug into her satchel.

*'sgot peppermint in it is what I hear.*

*Must help with the pain, if I can take a bit out?*

*Whatever you gotta do.*

*Hmm, this is quite thick,* Rosa pushed the thimbleful around her finger. *Do you know what else it is made of?*

*Dunno, I just warm the vial against me before I use it.*

*You said your boyfriend gave you this? What does his family do?*

*Well, just from the vial it's quite clear they got someone making medicine, and always be talking about herbs and bringing mead so I figure they do beekeeping.*

Rosa put the sample up to her nose.

*Beeswax, then.*

*Something about it?*

*Well, Diana, you see how it is greasy and stays sticky after you put it on?*

*Yeah, saw that in the morning. The inside takes the grease so the outside stays fresh.*

*Mhmm, that is smart. When did you start using it?*

*Yesterday, when the boy was last over.*

*Is he on the convoy?*

*Maybe, maybe not.*

*That's alright.*

Rosa pressed more at Diana's stump, and saw that the red had faded within the safety margin, though longer than she would have liked.

*Been like this for a long while.*

*Has it taken longer? Shorter?*

*Well, takes longer after tracking season, and shorter when I'm holed up in the blind.*

Rosa got her clipboard.

*Alright, and if you are comfortable, can you tell me how the amputation happened?*

Rosa could hear her own pen click.

*It's alright if you—*

*Crushing injury, when I was... twelve, I think.*

*I am sorry to hear that.*

*Dad brought me down the mountain, and, and, the vet helped me with it.*

*Okay, okay, that is all I need to know. Thank you for telling me.*

Diana's left hand combed her ponytail.

*So... give it to me straight, doc.*

*From what I am seeing, it is a nerve bundle that had grown in when the amputation happened. Nerves grow into a blob when they're cut, you see. And one is right here on the back left of your stump, just beneath the skin. And walking on it all day is what is hurting it and annoying you.*

*Can't you take it out?*

*That would be beyond what I can do. A surgeon is needed for this operation.*

*I see.*

*But, keep using the ointment if it helps, though make sure that you put it on before bed and to wipe it clean before you put the socket back on. It would be dangerous if you put it on, and then walked. The numbing makes you unable to feel if it is hurting too bad and you need to take a break. Any questions so far?*

*Where can I get a surgeon?*

*I can write you into the queue for the university hospital, but the waitlist is quite long, so I can't promise anything in the short term.*

*I see.*

*And, just to note, the time it took for the redness on your stump to fade was still within the safety margin, but towards the high end. See if you can adjust your workday so you walk less and spend more time in the blind. If the redness stays for more than ten minutes, come to the clinic at once.*

*Understood.*

*One last thing, try and get the formula off of your boyfriend.*

*What kind of doctor puts the easiest thing last?*

*Well, medical student, in my defence. Any questions?*

*Can't say so.*

Diana put her leg back on.

*Thank you, doc.*

*You are more than welcome, Diana.*

---

The next case came at lunch, when she was cutting bread for her butter, cheddar, colby, butter-fried egg and butter-fried onion sandwich. It was young Abe's birthday, so Rosa had him stick his tongue out and then dropped the tincture in. It apparently tasted like salt and a bit like mold. There was some apple juice in the ice box, courtesy of her predecessor, and she gave it to the child to wash it down. The fact that it kept him in observation for the next ten minutes was entirely incidental.

The family gave her four bottles of buttermilk as thanks.

She hung the sausage on the coat rack at the front of the clinic, and slept on the examining bed.
