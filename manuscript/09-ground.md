*Nadia, be sweet and grab me a bottle of soda from the ice-box before you head to the greenhouse?* Alex Tsien said, kneeling before a waist-high wall coming up on the family's side of the third-floor hallway.

*Here, and one for Lewis, when he comes down.*

*Thank you, tell the botanists I love whatever they're putting in these bottles.*

The elevator doors were perpetually open and there was a garden gate installed so people wouldn't fall in. Alex was there when they took the car out. What remained was a winch that reached the top floor, a cargo basket rated to carry 500 kilograms, and a label in bold font that said *DO NOT EXCEED 100 KG*. You drove it by sending a young man to the ground floor and hollering down the well *step on it, Lewis*! Nadia, of course, took the stairs.

It would appear that he cursed himself, under the breath, for forgetting to tell her to buy some new hinges on the way home.

A basket came down from the fifth floor. Lewis's voice came down the well. *Daaaad, bricks for ya.*

*Come down and help me with wall.*

They sat down and drank the soda first. But not before Alex went to the bathroom and ran the tap to a bucket. It was a honey-sweetened bug, with a smattering of whatever it was that Nadia helped grow in the greenhouses. It smelled faintly of oak.

*How are we gonna take out the threshold? and I found tiles to match our foyer on fifth.*

*Foyer?*

*A xuanguan.*

*Ah.*

*So, after this is done, we're gonna put the shoe rack on the new back-wall?*

*Might have to paint Nona's one black, like ours. The white is so dirtied.*

Water spilled onto the shower's floor.

*Now, help me with mixing the concrete.*

---

Collie came home sometime in the afternoon, and made the boys wash their hands before handing them their potato samosas. They had bits of carrot and onion in them, and were spiced with dill and caraway.

They took off their tools, and doused themselves in their clothes. Collie put her good dress back in the wardrobe and washed off her makeup before the sweat did it for her. Someone had ripped a knee off their jeans, and Nona was at the greenhouse so it was up to her to patch it.

Two t-shirts that were not grey in the morning found themselves in the hamper. Alex found Lewis a stool for laying the top of the wall. Two arms complained about a trowel's journey up and down and a scoop of paste and back up again. Someone pulled out a length of string to measure for the doorway they needed to nail together tomorrow.

Several pieces of 2x4s framed the doorway, the women had to duck coming home. Lewis said sorry and that it will be gone tomorrow, promise.

Father and son washed up with a towel, in the men's part of the river. Alex made sure that Lewis's hair was as dry as the towel could get it.

The nightstand held a beeswax candle, unscented.

*Alex.*

*Hmm? what is it, four-eyes?*

*The committee talked today.*

*On?*

*Moving the observatory, and they want me back up there to do the packing.*

*When are they sending you out?*

*We haven't decided yet, but they say it will be after the dry season.*

*And they must need an electrician, right?*

*They should.*

*You should tell Collie, then.*

*I will, in the morning, I just wanted to tell you first.* Maia hugged him tighter.

*There, there. We'll go up there together, hmm? A gun can't be that different from a nail gun.*

*I'm just, then Lewis will be here alone. And when he rides we won't know if he—*

*—He will have the cousins, the boys are careful, and Maddy is smart, she can keep him alive.*

*—but we won't know how long we will be away! He hardly had me, I can't take you away from him too.*

His hand covered hers, he could feel her nose and forehead on the small of his back. The candle flickered from a gust of wind.

*We could bring him too,* he pinched at the back of her middle finger, letting the skin slide off his fingertips.

*But—*

*An electrician needs an assistant.*

*And he will be away from the family.*

*And close to us, and another hand to pack things, and we will be out in no time.*

*But, won't it better, if, he is young, and, if we don't, Collie would—*

*We will, I promise. Our son has good eyes, your eyes.*

With his left hand, he held her hand in his; with a shuffle, his right palm smothered the light.

The shuffle continued, and turned him supine. The hand that found her chin was still warmer than blood. He cursed himself for getting the order backwards, though her lips were soft candlelit or not.

She shifted the held hands.

*Because I've been meaning to get us a proper gate, like how we had back in the old country. They knew their things.*

*Right, so, I'll want to find one next time I am out?*

*Ha! Just don't get left behind by the big boys.*

*Oh, Dad, they won't dare to leave a brother, much less a Swartz.*

*...Better be, now get the keys to the machinists, I'll get us all something chunky for dinner.*

Collie brought home a casserole, with thinly sliced potatoes, butter, and pickles on the side. A towel was formed into a donut shape, to hold the round-bottomed pot of corn chowder ladled from the dining hall. Mugs toasted with apple cider in them, to celebrate the new entrance. The tiles set into their places beneath a temporary plank.

The pot was labeled a wok, when a young Alex Tsien bought it from an Asian grocer three months after landing on this side of the planet. He also bought something made of mango and sago that day, for the train back to campus.

---

Lewis picked the lock on another apartment on the fifth floor. The shelves they found took a bit of finagling to tie to the cargo basket. The pair of radios he found on last week's run came in handy. Nona complained about the sawdust in the second living room, so they ended up building the rest of what Collie asked for outside the building, out of old pallets from the city.

Collie's room was on the west side of the apartment, and the afternoon would remind her of her duties outside of it. One of the women was working a hand crank to bring a basket of groceries up.

Someone knocked on her door. She closed a binder.

*Enter.*

*Doctor Swartz?* A head peeked in.

*By Jove, Alex! How long have we eaten from the same loaf, just Collie is fine.*

*...can never be too polite.*

*Walk the rest of yourself in, sit, tea?*

*Would be lovely.* The chair was upholstered in a pleather that had stayed remarkably intact.

*So, what is it?*

*Well, just, regarding the decision to expand the storeroom in the other side of our property, I had Nadia go to the archives, and there may be a weight concern.*

*How so?*

*I looked at the building's blueprints and age, nobody had been inspecting it for years...*

*And?*

*Nona and Nadia make honey, and it might be too heavy to put on our floor, grains, too.*

*Does Lewis have an opinion?*

*Not yet, he is still tearing things down on the fifth floor.*

*Well, the simplest would be to just buy as we need, and to keep the honey at the apiary,* Alex looked at Collie's face, *but I understand that something closer would be best for winter.*

*Yes, it would.*

*We would want the weight on the foundation, so we either build a new storehouse off the road, or, we wall off a chunk of the ground floor.*

*I will visit Epsilon, but in that case expect them next-next Monday.*

*Yes, ma'am.*

It was not just one.
