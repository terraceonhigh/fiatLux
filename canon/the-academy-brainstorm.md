# The Academy
### Working bible, third edition — descent removed, project installed

*Supersedes the second edition, which is in git history. Two things the second edition was architecturally built on are gone: **the descent to an 1880s floor**, and **the year-1080 frame narrative**. In their place the polity has a purpose — see §XII. The martyrology is cut; the millennium is not.*

*Sections marked **SETTLED** are closed unless reopened; **OPEN** sections are still yours.*

---

## 0. Register

**Nobledark. Tofu-firm.** Not grimdark, and — the likelier hazard on this side — not cosy.

The failure mode of nobledark is hagiography without cost: martyrs remembered, institution never made to choose. The corrective is not cruelty. It is **tragic choice** — every sacrifice is funded out of something, the something is named, and the people who voted against were not cowards. Keep the bodies; keep them in the minutes, where they would actually be.

**The purpose, which the second edition never stated:** the polity exists to keep human life as free of pain as possible. Not flourishing, not glory — **a floor**, and deliberately modest, because modest is what makes it *auditable*. The Academy can show its work: an estuary is poisoned because the factory averts more suffering than the estuary costs, and the number is written down. That is what "brutal but honest and upstanding arithmetic" means. Not that the answers are kind — that they are legible, defended in public, and recorded. The counter-argument needs no villain: the estuary was where people fished, and the ledger only ever learned to count one kind of pain.

Operating rules:

- **Villainy is out; cost is in.** No evil doctors. Doctors who must decide, in a farmyard, with insufficient supply.
- **Division counts, not decrees.** Where the polity does something terrible or magnificent, there was a vote, it was close, and the minutes survive.
- **The heroism should be small and technical.** The exemplar: the boat goes upstream to the factory for the product, and **the captain orders masks and goggles, and that is that.** No plaque, no pause. The poisoning survives as an operational rule and nothing else. This scales — prefer the log entry to the speech.
- **Exterior only.** Epiphany amputated. Load-bearing objects: the key, the sill, the ration scale, the master key, the lintel, the laminated card, the locker of perished mask straps.
- **The long horizon is native, not exceptional.** A supervisor's whole function is to make someone who will surpass them after they are dead. Rothamsted's grass plots, Beal's buried seed bottles, Ise Jingū's timber on a two-century rotation. "To die, not seeing the fruit" is the ordinary condition of institutional life; you have simply found the register in which it reads as glory rather than as a pension problem.
- **Entropy is the antagonist, not decline.** There is no second descent. A steady state above the sustainable floor is not preservation, it is continuous remanufacture at exactly the rate of wear. Bearings wear, seals perish, and in a maritime climate the damp gets into everything. Nobody *holds* a technology; they remake it, to tolerance, forever. The heroism is that the tolerance didn't slip.

## I. Settled

**Absorption, not deputisation.** The state retreats; the Academy picks up the slack because the functions were locally motivated to begin with. The food bank is your evidence, though note what it actually proves: a food bank is *alms* — discretionary, non-universal, no entitlement, no appeal. That is the pre-political form of welfare. **The threshold of statehood is bureaucratisation, not provision**: the moment relief acquires a criterion, a form, and a right of appeal. Somebody has to be told no and be able to contest it.

**And vouching is the pre-political form of citizenship.** The same structure one layer down. Before there is a register there is a laminated card, written in longhand and signed by two women at a kitchen table — discretionary, no criterion, no appeal, and honoured because of who signed it rather than what it says. The arc is already dramatised: refusal at the clinic on the grounds that the patient is neither student nor staff, then the fix, which is not to break the rule but to **absorb her employer** so the category changes. Membership by vouching is how these polities actually enrol people, and the precedent it sets is worse than either policy it replaced: the boundary is permeable *to people who know someone*.

Two consequences to keep. The lever that works is **category definition, not influence** — whoever defines who counts, counts. And the precedent generalises badly: once *absorb the employer* works, every unaffiliated professional with a friend inside tries it, so the Academy walks into the Winters carrying departments it cannot fund and staff it cannot feed. **The famine is worse because of kindness.**

**Exit is the hinge.** The apocalypse grants the Academy no new powers whatsoever. Everything it does to members is legitimated by the fact that they can leave — conduct code, residence searches, withheld transcripts, all resting on consent-by-continued-enrolment. Collapse removes exit, and the existing powers become sovereign powers *by that subtraction alone*. You need no usurpation scene. The conduct code is word-for-word identical on both sides: a code of membership on Monday, criminal law on Friday, because there is nowhere left to be expelled to. Rustication stops being an inconvenient year at home and becomes exposure. The penal code inflates in severity without a single vote being taken.

**Seed crystal — and the metaphor is not benign.** A seed crystal contributes lattice, not substance; the solution was already supersaturated. What the Academy supplies is *geometry*. So the polity inherits the shape of the template rather than the shape of the problem. Jurisdiction follows faculty boundaries where the boundaries are nonsense. The year is divided into terms and the harvest is scheduled around them, everyone knows this is idiotic, and nobody can propose otherwise, because the calendar is what the institution *is*. Reading week falls in the middle of the lambing.

Corollary worth having: **the template has no concept of territory, only of membership.** So the Academy governs its district superbly and its region badly, because it cannot conceive of a citizen who never enrolled. It supplies geometry, and the geometry has no suburbs in it.

**Endowment is a legal regime, not a synonym for holdings.** Corpus and income are distinct; the corpus may not be spent; the trustee's duty runs to a beneficiary. The Academy therefore inherits a land tenure that *forbids alienation*, and the interim fiction acquires teeth — a trustee holding property for a sovereign that no longer exists cannot sell it, and the lawyers will say so. Then famine. **Invading the corpus** is the term of art and the best constitutional crisis available: breaking the endowment is the precise moment the Academy admits it is a state rather than a trust. It will find a way to do it while insisting otherwise. Probably by reclassifying the land as an instructional facility.

**The interim fiction.** Senate declares it exercises civil functions in trust, pending restoration of lawful authority. The interim never ends. Permanent factions for free: **Restorationists** (the fiction is binding and forbids certain acts), **Sovereigntists** (drop the pretence; called barbarians), and the administrative middle who find the fiction convenient and therefore sacred.

**Bureaucracy before coercion.** You have inverted the usual order. High infrastructural power, low despotic power: superb at counting people, incompetent at killing them, no institutional memory of violence. Its first real war is a catastrophe of a specific kind — perfectly administered.

**The bookstore is a separate corporation.** A subsidiary is not the trust. Everything the endowment forbids — alienating land, pledging assets, arming men, binding the Academy to foreign settlements — the Academy cannot do and the store can, and the Senate discovers this with enormous relief. Chartered company, exactly: the flag follows the factory, each affiliated village gets a store before it gets a court, and the grain receipts are redeemed there, so the currency circulates through a commercial subsidiary rather than the Bursar. The merchant marine belongs to the store for the same reason, and its ships carry guns because merchant vessels always did, and nobody calls it a navy for about eighty years. Nationalising the bookstore is the constitutional crisis of the third generation, fought as a question of procurement.

**The empire is an accreditation empire — but the register is *respublica literaria*, not vassalage.** Sovereignty as gradient rather than border, legitimacy travelling inside people, withdrawal of recognition as the ultimate and once-a-century weapon. Correspondence between distant institutions carried by whoever is sailing that way, three months each direction, letters arriving after their authors' deaths. The observatory keeps the twenty-metre band open because skip does not require anything in between. Colleagueship, not charter.

**This is no longer only a register. It is the method** — see §XII.

**Naming is the martyrology machinery, and it is already installed.** Endowed chairs, named lectures, plaques, buildings — a university is a calendar of saints that has forgotten it is one. First edition said every institution would be named for a donor whose money came from something nobody discusses. Inverted, the mechanism is untouched: **the polity names its founders after people the old world took from and did not credit.** The manuscript already runs it — Elbakyan, Latimer. **Say it once and never explain it.**

Note what the mechanism is *not*, now that the saints are cut: there is no fixed roster, no canonical list of the venerated, and no one whose name you are obliged to use. The corrective naming works better with a founding generation that is *un*-corrected. The Academy that eventually canonises the uncredited was built by women with Latinate names, and the correction had to be made against something.

**And the naming obeys the same law as everything else here.** Two founding practices — identity documents and food security — both entered the world as **academic hearsay**, whispered between colleagues who already vouched for each other, deniable, with no minute and no recoverable origin. A letter of introduction is eight hundred years old and academics never stopped writing them; nobody reinvented anything. Expect the same of any custom the polity later treats as constitutional: someone reached for a habit, and it hardened.

## II. Still open

Four of the second edition's seven are now closed.

1. ~~Governance template~~ — **SETTLED: Commonwealth.** §IV already presupposed it, writing a doge *elected for life by Convocation*. The American template would mean building Convocation from nothing after having already given the Chancellor to it.
2. ~~Speed of collapse~~ — **SETTLED: slow.** Decades, not years. The richer spectacle: an institution watching itself become a government while insisting it isn't. See §VI.
3. **The ratio** (§III) — **OPEN.** 1:10 remains the recommendation and nothing has settled it.
4. ~~Coal within 100 km~~ — **SETTLED: yes, and by water.** Which matters less for supply than for reach: the field sits at the *edge* of the hundred-kilometre coercion radius, so the industrial base is at the precise limit of what the Academy can compel. Annex it early at a cost the Bursar cannot fund, in the one eight-week window per year when force is possible at all — or hold it by permanent negotiation forever, which means the whole industrial base sits with a partner it cannot coerce.
5. ~~Corridor or blob~~ — **SETTLED: corridor.** Linear along navigable water, bulk economy coastal, the store's wavefront moving by schooner. Ungoverned interstices are mountain and inlet.
6. ~~Polis or empire~~ — **DISSOLVED, not settled.** See §XII. They stay small in population and reach far in relation; expansion is diplomatic and epistemic rather than territorial. The face-to-face polity survives its own ambition — but the ambition still threatens it, differently. See §VIII.
7. **The two-tier credential** — **OPEN**, and now interacts with §XII, because a standards union has to decide whose certificates count.

## III. The premise, corrected

**"By happenstance"** — discard. Universities sit on bluffs beside rivers next to farmland because that is where cheap peripheral land was in 1870, and because agricultural colleges were legally required to hold arable. If your Academy began as a School of Agriculture and Mechanic Arts, the farm is inside the walls, the veterinary school is a going concern, the seed bank is institutional property, and the extension service already has agents in every village within forty miles. Free history. Take it. **A land-grant charter also makes military instruction a founding obligation**, so the militia is the officer training corps outliving the army that used to take its graduates.

**The binding constraint is demography — at the start.** A university *begins* as a cohort rather than a population: eighty percent aged 18–24, no children, few elderly, ratios set by admissions rather than nature. Left alone that ages into a single grey bolus. So the Academy must absorb the countryside, and the credentialed are then a minority ruling a majority within one generation.

But it does become a population, and quickly. **These people live and love and have children and die.** Grandmothers on the Grounds, pregnancies that change a rota, a funeral that also creates a vacancy somebody has to cover, a child asleep under the bench in the chronometer workshop because childcare is whoever is on shift. It is not a monastery with families; **it is a town whose civic religion happens to be an institution.** The Rule does the load-bearing work — horarium, apprenticeship, maintenance as assigned labour on a timetable — and then everyone walks home from the shift to a household. The enclosure is not a calling. The family stays the way a family stays in a mill town: the work is there, the housing is there, and nobody has ever framed it as devotion. Devotion would be a monument.

| Ratio (matriculated : hinterland) | Polity |
|---|---|
| 1:2 | Broad-based republic. Credentialism erodes fast. Least interesting. |
| 1:10 | Venetian oligarchy. Sustainable, paranoid, litigious. **Best fit for the register.** |
| 1:40 | Conquest aristocracy. Beautiful, unstable — but hard to keep out of grimdark. |

## IV. Titles

Rule: **the name is sticky, the function drifts, and the drift is set by the office's material substrate.** Half of these are semantic homecomings; the academy borrowed its vocabulary from church and burgh, and under pressure the words go back where they came from.

**Dyarchy.** *Chancellor* stays ceremonial and hardens into a doge — elected for life by Convocation, signs everything, decides nothing, occasionally decisive. *Vice-Chancellor* is the executive. The gap between them is where every constitutional crisis happens. *Provost*: a Scottish burgh's provost is its mayor. Give the Provost the courts.

**Organs.** *Registrar* holds the rolls and therefore the census, citizenship, cadastre, licensure — the interior ministry by inertia. *Bursar* is the mint. *Proctors* are police with no retooling; they had constables already. *Parking Services* controls movement and holds the vehicle fleet: customs and excise, a joke that gets less funny the longer you look. *Advancement* maintains relationships with people who are elsewhere and asks them periodically for favours — the foreign ministry, and also the mobilisation register, since it holds the alumni rolls. *Superintendent of Grounds* is Warden of the Marches, and note that "the Grounds" is already the word for the territory. *Custodian* — *custos*, guardian. Facilities is a guild with apprenticeships, secrets, and keys.

**The gendarmerie splits along descent and stays split.** *Proctors* out of campus security — unarmed, disciplinary, handling members, and their sanctions are academic and social because in this polity exclusion means no ration card. *Field Force* out of an absorbed garrison — armed, external, handling everyone else. Different uniforms, different recruitment, mutual contempt, neither abolishable. And how a university digests an army is the only way it knows: **it credentials it.** The soldiers become instructors with academic rank, the militia becomes a faculty with a syllabus and a hood and a place in the sumptuary code, and men who would never accept a Senate's authority will accept a chair and precedence in a procession.

**Judiciary.** The Office of Student Conduct becomes the criminal courts: panels, standards of proof, appeals, all pre-built. *Peer review* recovers its Magna Carta sense. The Ethics Board decides what may be done to persons and is the quietest and most frightening body on the Grounds.

**Land and lineage.** *Tenure* — *tenere*, to hold. Academic tenure reverting to land tenure is the best etymological joke available and you should not resist it. Endowed chairs are named fiefs. Doctoral supervision is patron and client; academic genealogies are tracked heraldically already and become literal descent in two generations. *Emeriti* are a gerontocratic upper chamber and are worse than you expect.

**Classes.** Sessionals and adjuncts: literate, credentialed, landless, aggrieved — every pamphlet in the setting is written by someone teaching four sections a term in year eleven. The *Ombudsperson* is tribune of the plebs: beloved office or coward's sinecure, one per century.

**And the collapse-era rank is *Acting*.** When people leave and are not replaced, someone is made Acting Head, and in a healthy institution that ends after a year. Here it doesn't. A career is a sequence of acting appointments that stick — asked to cover, repeatedly, until the coverage is the post. Nobody is promoted; nobody is ever not covering for someone. Same permanent-provisionality mechanism as the interim fiction, arriving early, in one woman's job title.

**Ritual.** Academic dress is a complete sumptuary code — colour by faculty, cut by degree, hood as heraldry. The mace is a weapon that became a symbol and becomes a weapon again. *Rustication* is literally sending to the countryside and becomes exactly that. *Sabbatical* + Leviticus = jubilee: every seventh year, debts forgiven, fields fallow, nothing enforced. Residences already have Wardens; make the hall the deme, the regiment, the kitchen.

## V. Faculties into ministries

**Four conversion routes, and nothing else works.** A department converts if it holds a **substrate** (plant, herd, kiln, press), a **gate** (a licence others need), **clients** (an existing external caseload), or supplies the **myth**. A department with only a subject matter becomes a charity case, and charity cases are where the adjunct-Jacobins come from.

So the prestige inversion is **asset-holding up, asset-less down** — an axis that cuts through sciences and humanities alike, not between them.

- Molecular biology is high-status and dies in four years: reagent-consuming discipline, no reagent industry.
- **Printmaking owns a press.** A press makes currency, forms, and proclamations. Ceramics makes crucibles, insulators, pipe. Sculpture has a foundry. The art faculty holds more industrial capacity than physics.
- Physics survives only where it shades into **astronomy**, and astronomy survives because defining the calendar is a sovereign function. That is the observatory's institutional basis, not sentiment — and in a famine someone will move to shut it and reallocate the fuel, and the argument that saves it is **planting dates**, decided in committee on grounds nobody finds romantic.
- **Geography** is underrated: whoever holds the surveys holds the cadastre, and the cadastre is the tax base.
- **Education** is the most underrated of all: teacher certification is the transmission mechanism of the accreditation empire. Chartering a village school means certifying its teachers, which means the Faculty of Education writes the curriculum by which every child learns who they are. More durable than the militia, and nobody in the setting will think it prestigious.
- Faculties fracture internally more violently than they compete externally. Engineering becomes three ministries that loathe each other, and Electrical holds the cogen plant.
- **Chemistry keeps everything, forever** — arsenal, pharmacy, water authority, and the dye. A concentration nobody planned and nobody can unwind. Its chlor-alkali plant is the one that kills an estuary, and it kills it to keep the wells clean.
- **Classics turns out to hold the recipes.** Not government — recipes. Saponification from grease and wood ash, taught to children as a trick for making enormous bubbles, by someone who read the ancient world's technology and does not notice she is running a curriculum. The department with no survival function has the oldest manuals.
- Social scientists hold none of the four. They advise. Permanently.

**Requisition rule of thumb:** if outsiders can run it, the department loses it; if it needs tacit skill, the department becomes the ministry. The dairy is taken away.

**The permanent fight this sets up.** Facilities is a guild, and a guild's power is that its knowledge is *tacit and unpublished*. The Academy's creed says explain it, write it down, submit it to check. So the institution's central commitment stands in structural conflict with the department that runs the steam plant; both are behaving rationally; the Grounds cannot function without either. That is a coup risk with a motive that isn't ambition — see §VI Phase 5.

## VI. Chronology

**Phase 0 — Antecedent.** Land grant, farm, water licence, cogeneration, the EOC binder nobody has read. Everything needed is already owned and forgotten.

**The decay runs across four decades before anything looks like a founding**, and two clocks run at different speeds: the supply chain fails from the top of the complexity stack downward, the state fails from the periphery inward. Decade one, replacement stops and everything gets repaired instead. Decade two, the accords — function devolved a level at a time with grants declining in real terms, each step legal and minuted and framed as subsidiarity, and the Academy *wins contracts* and issues a press release. Decade three, no new procurement anywhere, digital registries come back from outages partial, the campus clinic starts seeing non-students and calls it community engagement and means it. Decade four, the professional migration: municipal and provincial staff arrive because the Grounds are walkable and still paying, increasingly in kind.

**And the interment, which is decade four's real business.** The arts and sciences that cannot survive without a planetary supply base are **buried, not lost** — by people who know what a supply chain was, who can say precisely why molecular biology cannot continue, and who can be cross-examined. It is committee work, not a moment. Departments are asked to state honestly what their discipline needs from outside the region, permanently; the honest ones say *everything*; boxes are packed correctly, labelled, inventoried, shelved. People cry in offices and then finish the inventory.

The archive therefore has **two rooms and two kinds of silence** — what was interred and will never be needed, and the negative record of what was tried and must never be repeated. The libraries stand vigil at the gates. And burying a science rather than losing it is a **promise**, against a day that will not come by itself: which is why every generation somebody moves to reduce the archive's heating allocation, and every generation somebody stands and says no. The most boring recurring item in a thousand years of minutes, and the one that decides everything. See §XII — the project is the promise being kept.

**Phase 1 — The Emergency.** The binder is opened and becomes the provisional government by default. First sovereign act is exclusion. Recommend the gate decision be a real cost with a known number, re-litigated by every reform movement for two centuries — but in this register it is a *decision under time pressure with insufficient information*, not a crime of the heart.

**Phase 2 — The Winters.** Caloric crisis. Note the shape: **a distribution failure, not a production failure.** The climate is mild and there is food in the ground a hundred kilometres away; what fails is the ability to move it. So the famine is maddening rather than elemental, and the first emergency act is not rationing but **commandeering the fleet** — the university's own buses, sent out for cabbages with the route number still on the front.

Meal plan becomes ration card; the Bursar becomes food dictator and writes a scale he knows is a lie he intends to correct in the spring. Athletics becomes the militia. First trial under Student Conduct procedure, with minutes.

**And the founding vote.** Its specifics are yours; its *type* is fixed. A close division, funded out of something named, minutes surviving by the luck of a clerk nobody remembers, and a losing side that was not cowardly — ideally with one member who voted against and bore the cost anyway. **The anniversary the polity keeps is the vote, not any death.** Two candidates are already built in the manuscript: the board taking up the water quality contract, and the vote to absorb the charity.

Underneath it, the arithmetic solving itself in public: the tens of thousands who were enrolled and are no longer here. **That is the Winters' actual function.**

**Phase 3 — The Reading.** The interim fiction is adopted. Factions crystallise.

**Phase 4 — Affiliation.** Extension service becomes the instrument of the province, because it always was. Circuit begins as practicum. Villages sign. Honorary degrees incorporate local strongmen — ennoblement by hood and Latin citation, which makes a rival into a subordinate without a shot.

**Phase 5 — First native generation.** Children who never saw the old world and find Restorationism absurd. Convocation hardens into an assembly. Credentialism becomes hereditary in practice while remaining meritocratic in statute. First serious coup attempt, from Facilities or the militia, because those hold what the Senate cannot replace — and note the motives are not ambition: Facilities defends guild secrecy, the absorbed garrison does not believe a committee should command it.

**Phase 6 — The republic of letters.** Charters, correspondence, fosterage, the store's wavefront. Wars, but few and expensive.

**Phase 7 — The long project.** Complexity is preserved at a cost the province pays; marginal return goes negative; the reform party proposes vocationalisation and is called barbarian, the conservative party proposes rigour and is called parasitic, and both are correct.

**The resolution is neither descent nor victory.** It is the decision to reach outward — because the binding constraint is planetary and cannot be solved inside a corridor. Phase 6's correspondence was the precondition; Phase 7 is where it acquires *intent*. The motion passes with amendments, as everything here does, and nobody in the room thinks they are founding a foreign policy.

*Phases 8, 9 and 10 of the second edition — absorption into a successor state, the dark stretch, the year-1080 restoration — are cut. The polity holds continuously. Note what that costs: the setting has traded its jeopardy for its ambition, and the "it stopped and someone restarted it" fact is gone. §XII carries the stake now.*

## VII. The province

**Circuit.** Practicum retrofitted as assize. The eyre did not displace manorial courts by decree; it out-competed them, offering enforceable writs, a jury of presentment, and above all a decision that could be appealed above the man who made it. Your rider hears disputes whether or not anyone authorised it, because a stranger with a form becomes an arbitrator the moment two people want one. **Appeal is the most addictive political technology ever devised.** The headman who invites the circuit in to settle a score is importing the instrument that makes headmen redundant.

**The graded archive.** Field intelligence is coursework, marked in red and filed, and therefore distorted in a known direction — successful interventions overreported, refusals underreported, because a village that would not cooperate reads on the transcript as a student who could not manage one. Nobody lies; everyone writes toward the marker. The picture is systematically optimistic for a century and the correction arrives as a rising in a district the file rated well-affiliated.

**The ratchet.** First visit the doctors are a gift and the grain is a request; third visit a service and a fee; ninth visit a schedule with an arrears column. No village can name the year it became a tax. **Medicine creates dependency grain never does** — they can grow food elsewhere; they cannot produce a surgeon.

**The clinical debt.** The teaching circuit needs bodies as much as the village needs treatment, and the village pays in grain and again in pathology, and only the first payment is written down. In your register this is not a hidden crime but an **acknowledged and unresolved debt** — argued in Senate, partly reformed, never closed, and the reason the consent forms in the third century are four pages long and in the vernacular. The legitimating myth is *true*: every Provost has done a rural rotation and remembers it as the most morally legible year of their life. A true myth cannot be dismantled with a pamphlet, and should not be.

**Triage is a sovereign act.** The decision on the exception, made in a yard by someone not yet licensed to make it. That, and no treaty, is the Academy's founding moment over a population that never consented to membership. The family of whoever was not chosen keeps the name for four generations.

**Empire by invitation.** Resist passive settlements. A headman calls the Academy in to settle a score; a faction wants affiliation precisely because it freezes their advantage. Most empires are joined more often than imposed — and the Academy will find expansion astonishingly easy and misread the ease as consent.

**What the polis actually owes the province**, filtered by what only something large, literate and permanent can do. Diagnosis, because identifying a blight needs a herbarium and a specimen library going back generations, and a blight named early is a famine prevented. **Veterinary medicine first in practice** — an ox is a family's traction, and the rider who saves the plough team carries none of the clinical circuit's moral horror. **Risk pooling**, which is the deepest item: not more food but *smoothing*, twelve valleys' good years covering one valley's failure, because where the gap between hardship and death is one bad harvest, variance reduction beats yield. Registered title and vital records, so a claim survives its holder and an inheritance dispute settles without knives. The calendar and the almanac. Certified teachers, midwives, surveyors. And the unglamorous infrastructure — a well sited correctly and a latrine sited downstream of it, drainage tile, a fire pump, water testing.

Which produces the finding nobody likes: **sanitation beats medicine** on suffering averted per unit of effort, by a wide margin, and every public health officer knows it. The strict arithmetic says build the pipe works, not the pharmacy. These are scholars, so **the drains get funded and the numbers go in the minutes** — and the wall stays physicians, and nobody finds that a contradiction worth resolving. You cannot put a well on a wall.

**Aesthetic:** municipal, not medieval. A light bar and a high-vis vest are the heraldry.

## VIII. Reach and scale

**Five radii, and they do not nest.**

| Function | Range |
|---|---|
| Bulk extraction | 50 km overland (draft animals eat the cargo); 300 km+ on navigable water at a tenth the cost |
| Coercion | 100 km, and only in the eight weeks after harvest — **every rebellion has a correct month** |
| Circuit administration | 250 km, bounded by the term calendar, because the rider must be back to mark |
| Commerce | Far. Medicine, seed and dye are value-dense; the store outruns the state |
| Legitimacy | Effectively unbounded, nearly free, travels inside people |

**Bridges set the boundary.** Asphalt is unmaintainable and gone in decades, but roadbeds and cuts persist; spans do not, and rebuilding one is beyond you. Civil Engineering decides which four crossings are maintained, and that committee decision **is** the territory, redrawn each generation by people who think they are allocating labour. On a corridor the same is true of **the ferry timetable**: the schedule is the territory, and a household boat working the gaps between sailings is either licensed, tolerated, or quietly competing.

**The pre-industrial numbers.** 50 km radius ≈ 7,800 km²; ~25 people/km² post-collapse ≈ 150–200,000 souls at full recovery. Extraction at 5–15% supports a metropole of **three to six thousand**. (Attica, roughly.)

**The ~1900 equilibrium.** Not a ceiling they peak at before falling — **what they hold indefinitely, by remanufacture.** Three multipliers compound. Yields roughly double (superphosphate is Chemistry's, tile drainage is Ceramics', mechanical reaping breaks the harvest-labour bottleneck) to ~45/km²; **nitrogen remains the hard cap**, since Haber is 1913 and needs high-pressure vessels, catalysts and enormous continuous energy — which makes nitrogen upstream of the tech ceiling itself, not merely of the dinner table. Extraction roughly doubles to 15–25%, and this is the underrated one: pre-modern states under-extracted because they could not *see*, and yours has a cadastre, a census, literacy and standard weights. **Legibility is worth more than tax collectors.** Haulage falls an order of magnitude on rail. Compounded: base ~2 million, metropole 50–80,000.

What sets that level is not knowledge but three things: how many distinct trades stay warm in a population that size, materials purity, and **which elements lie inside the corridor** — because alloy steels need chromium and nickel and tungsten, catalysts need platinum, cells need mercury. Their tech level is a geology question wearing a lab coat. The one capability that regenerates itself from hand tools is the **machine tool chain**: three plates rubbed in rotation give a true flat surface from nothing, and from a surface plate comes every instrument in the setting. Lose the shop and it can be rebuilt in a generation from the method; lose the method and no surviving machinery saves you, because machines wear.

**And the only route past ~1900 is a restored planetary division of labour** — which is precisely why §XII exists.

**The shape gets worse as it grows.** Rail is corridors, not discs — 30 km either side and you are back to cart economics. The territory is a dendrite with ungoverned interstices, and every rising begins by lifting rail and cutting wire. The telegraph decouples information from transport and quietly kills the circuit rider, replacing him with a resident magistrate who can be instructed same-day: an employee rather than a semi-autonomous agent. **The practicum-as-intelligence apparatus dies with him.**

**The cost to weigh hardest.** At three thousand members the polity is face-to-face: the Senate genuinely deliberates, reputation is total, committee judgment substitutes for law. At fifteen thousand citizens down four corridors, none of it holds — you need representation, standing statute, and a professional bureaucracy that is not the faculty, and the conduct code cannot try murders any more. **Every charming thing in this setting is a function of smallness.**

The second edition named 1880s technology as the solvent. **Redirect the threat: the solvent is the project.** Territorial growth was never the danger, because §XII's expansion is diplomatic rather than territorial. What dissolves the face-to-face polity is the *obligations* — a standards union to administer, correspondents owed answers, expeditions to fund, treaties whose terms outlive the people who signed them. Same tragedy, different cause, and it stays live rather than resolved.

**Salvage is a stock.** For the first century the Academy needs scrapyards, not mines — the ruins are refined metal already smelted, the richest ore body in history, and the geologists survey cities rather than rock. Second transition around the fourth generation, from wrecking to extraction. If the ore lies outside the dendrite, you have acquired a dependency and a foreign policy.

**Two reversals of the first edition.** Capital-intensive polities lose to territorial states *at parity*; 1900 against pre-industrial is not Venice. The Academy can plausibly prevent anyone else forming a state — which makes technological containment a live plank of foreign policy, and puts it in direct contradiction with §XII, which needs other polities to be *capable* partners. And rotary press + wood-pulp newsprint + mass literacy + a fostered elite holding second-class diplomas is Anderson's recipe exactly: print gives peripheral nationalism its vehicle, third generation, on schedule.

## IX. Strategy: the first twenty-five years

**This is the strongest the Academy will ever be.** The technical monopoly is a *stock*, not a flow — drugs expire, reagents run out, bearings wear, and the twelve people who can machine a rifled barrel are in their fifties. Doctrine reduces to one line: **convert depreciating stock into durable structure before the stock is gone.** Trade antibiotics for a permanent circuit court, not for grain. Trade a pump for the right to site and maintain the pump forever. Every transaction should leave an institution rather than a satisfied customer — and the advisors who understand this will be in perpetual conflict with the Bursar, who is trying to survive the winter and can price grain but cannot price a precedent.

The twelve machinists are the two-generation problem in miniature, and it is not fixable by writing things down: generation one knows which steps in the protocol matter and which are ritual, generation three has the book and cannot tell the difference. Yield falls and nobody can say why. **The protocol is not the skill.** See §XII on what to do about it.

Cheap plays, because expensive ones are unaffordable: be promiscuous with recognition (honours cost nothing and make you the fount of legitimacy); standardise metrology (whoever defines the bushel adjudicates every commercial dispute without sending anyone); charter rivals rather than fight them; never let one valley become the sole grain supplier.

**Faculty disagreement tracks disciplinary training with depressing fidelity.** IR preaches urgency and balancing. Anthropology alone knows the periphery, counsels working through kinship, is dismissed as having gone native, and is right at least twice. Economics advises extraction through terms of trade because price is invisible in a way requisition is not, and is catastrophically wrong about the strategic reserve exactly once. History supplies the long view, is useless in the room, and is vindicated in the archive. Standing failure mode across all of them: they model the periphery as **states** — unitary actors with interests — because that is what they were trained to model, and it is kin networks and factions, so the Academy keeps signing agreements with men who cannot deliver and being genuinely puzzled.

**The correct policy, which they lose.** The constraint is demographic, so aggressive naturalisation — mid-career admission, mass matriculation of useful outsiders, the honorary degree used freely and cheaply — fixes the ratio *and* strips rivals of the people they would need to form a state. Highest return available, near-zero cost. It fails because it is a franchise question and the credentialed minority will not vote to dilute itself. The demographers write the correct memo in year six; it circulates; it is praised; it is referred to committee. The realists get their militia instead: affordable, visible, addressed to the wrong constraint.

## X. Knowing things

The service is not founded, it is **collated**. The streams already flow — graded district reports, alumni correspondence, admissions interviews conducted in the field, examiners travelling to score entrance papers. What is missing is that nobody reads them against each other. The apparatus begins the day someone asks the Library to index the district files, and the founder is whoever notices that two students described the same valley incompatibly. Historically accurate: naval intelligence divisions began as geographical handbooks compiled by academics. **Registry precedes operations everywhere.** Your first spymaster is a cataloguer with no interest in power, which is why she is given the job.

Same law gives you the registry of the dead. A death needs a physician's certificate, the provincial office consolidates and backlogs, and some records clerk starts keeping carbons in a binder *in case*. Then a funeral home asks, then a bank, then a court. **The Academy becomes the registry because it never threw anything away** — which is also why a paper institution outlives a digital state, and why the first great forgetting happens with the lights still on, as licensed databases go dark and proprietary formats stop being readable.

**Tasking is the threshold** — the moment the collating office specifies in advance what it wants observed, the practicum stops being education and becomes cover, and the students are the last to know. Admissions is the recruitment arm unmodified. Advancement is the handling arm, because maintaining warm contact with a distant person, asking periodically for a favour, and recording it all in a file is not *like* agent handling. The Ethics Board becomes the warrant authority: a body that licenses is a body that conceals, and the oversight and the enabling are the same committee.

**Compartmentation is the constitutional break.** Everything the Academy is rests on knowledge that circulates — published, cited, held in trust. An intelligence product is restricted, unattributable, unfalsifiable. The first report stamped for limited distribution is a foreign organ in the body, and the Academy is now doing internally what it does externally: teaching operation while withholding replication. The anthropologists tear themselves apart over it, on schedule, with justification.

**And they will reach for the gate to do it.** You cannot be a city on a hill and also keep secrets. The first restricted report is a lamp put under a bushel, and the people arguing about it will use that exact phrase, because it is carved on the gate. **The intelligence service is a theological problem before it is a political one.**

**Two permanent frictions.** The Registrar fights the Library for ownership of the file, and the winner determines whether the service sits under the interior ministry or floats free. And counterintelligence is unsolvable by design: the heirs of every rival valley are in your capital with four years' run of the place. Surveilling them poisons the fosterage, which is the product; not surveilling them means the procedures are known everywhere. No clean answer, only a policy reversed with each Chancellor.

**Structural weakness it cannot train out:** magnificent at basic intelligence — the gazetteer, the genealogies, the soil surveys, the finest provincial handbook anyone has compiled — and hopeless at warning, because scholarship optimises for the considered judgment and warning requires the timely one. Peer review takes a season. The rising takes three weeks.

## XI. Memory

*The second edition's canon block and saints list are cut. There is no fixed roster of the venerated, no Division at a stated tally, no named martyr at a sill, no lecture series with a number, no Founders' Wall. The naming **device** survives in §I; the roster does not. Two items survive here, and both are load-bearing.*

**The ballad wins.** Around two centuries in, a Literature faculty reconstructs the founding in verse, because the archive is minutes and minutes do not sing. Bad history, exact emotionally, taught to every child in the province. The historians publish a devastating and entirely correct rebuttal, and lose. **The poem is what the polity means by itself.**

***Fiat Lux* was cut by the cogeneration crew in year seven of the old university as a joke about the turbine hall.** They meant electrical light. Every generation since has taken it for scripture — and the crew were *correct*, which is the whole point. Keeping the lights on is the mission, literally and entirely. The sacred fire of this order is a boiler somebody feeds at three in the morning, and the vigil is a shift. See `research/the-two-lights.md`: the motto carries two duties that pull opposite ways — **shine outward** and **tend inward** — and every institutional argument for a thousand years is which one gets the fuel this year.

That is also the law by which anything here becomes canonical: **a joke hardens into scripture.** Applies to the motto, to the affiliation letters, to whatever the ballad decides the founding was.

## XII. Is it sustainable? — and the project

Yes, better than the genre allows, and not for reasons anyone inside will enjoy.

Caloric closure is a non-problem — a city-state feeds from a province, not from its walls. Military survival is **exogenous**: city-states persist where neighbours stay small, so the Academy's fate turns on regional fragmentation rather than on anything it does well.

**The binding constraint is material replacement**, and no polity this size reproduces a modern technological base. **The minimum viable population for most of what the Academy does is the planet.**

The second edition read that as a death sentence and derived a descent from it. **Invert the conclusion. If the minimum viable population is the planet, the Academy's answer is to reassemble the planet.**

Not by conquest. By **correspondence, certification and re-linking** — which means everything already in this bible turns out to be an instrument of a project nobody named:

- **The republic of letters is the method**, not a register. Correspondence between surviving institutions, three months each direction, is how a world system gets rebuilt.
- **The accreditation empire is a standards union.** A restored global order needs shared metrology, shared time, shared credentials — and authentication is what this polity actually exports. Potency, weights, time, a seal, a recognizable fist on a key. The medicine is nearly incidental; what a distant city is offered is *a party whose claims can be checked*.
- **The observatory's open band is the project's ear.** Listening for other survivors. Receive is nearly free and transmit is dear, which is what makes the observatory a standards institution rather than a participant: it is the one party that can answer. Its silence is then a diplomatic event with nobody at fault, because the band closes on the solar cycle and it keeps calling into it.
- **The voyage is the project's first outward act** — penicillin carried to a city last heard three months ago, which is reconnaissance for reassembly rather than charity. Note the verification problem runs both ways: the call cannot be confirmed without sailing, and the receiving city cannot know the medicine is real, which is why the ship carries the assay standard and someone who can demonstrate potency on a plate in a foreign harbour.
- **§II.6 dissolves.** Small in population, far in relation. Expansion is diplomatic and epistemic, so the face-to-face polity survives its own ambition — though see §VIII for what the obligations do to it instead.

**And the interment becomes the promise the project is trying to keep.** They buried what needs a planet, against a day they could not bring about alone. Every generation that funds the archive's heating is voting, without saying so, that the day is still coming. Whether it does is the open question of the setting, and it should stay open.

**The strongest survival mechanism is one the advisors will not recognise:** the **examination**, the most durable elite-reproduction technology ever built, roughly thirteen centuries of service, working by co-opting exactly the people who would otherwise lead the revolt. It solves circulation, which is what kills ordinary aristocracies. But it works by *decoupling* — the exam persists while its content ossifies into something formal, difficult and useless, and that is not a defect but the reason it sorts cleanly. Test something practical, get lobbying about the syllabus. Test something beautiful and dead, get a mandarinate.

**Which contradicts the anti-drift creed, and the contradiction should stay in.** A monastery preserves a practice; a university preserves the *why*. Drift is what happens to a practice transmitted without its reasons, so the corrective is an enforced **explanation requirement**: you may not receive a process unless you can say what each step is for. But a recited explanation is still a ritual, so the only robust test is rederivation without the protocol in front of you — which means permitting failure, which means a spoiled batch, which in a polity whose purpose is minimising pain is **an annual line item in the budget of suffering**, voted on and never comfortable. Cheapest and least glamorous companion institution: a statutory **negative archive** of what does not work, recording failure as a duty rather than an embarrassment.

So the exam sorts and the explanation requirement preserves, and they want opposite things from a syllabus, and the Senate argues about it forever. Both are right about different problems. Do not resolve it.

**Fosterage cuts identically:** binding the heirs laterally to the metropole is what prevents any valley consolidating into the territorial rival that would end the setting. The vulnerability and the survival mechanism are the same policy.

## XIII. Still to decide

Beyond §II.3 and §II.7:

1. **The gate on the first night.** Number turned away, if any. Still open, and heavier than it was: the manuscript has built the affiliation machinery in detail — the card, the clinic refusal, the absorption — so whatever happens at the gate is that machinery's first real use, and everyone in the corridor learns what the boundary is permeable to.
2. **Does the restoration succeed?** Deliberately open, as posed. Note that the answer need never appear.
3. **Indigenous nations are co-sovereigns and the bible has no account of them.** This is the largest structural gap remaining. §I makes withdrawal of recognition the empire's ultimate weapon — and it only bites against a party that *needs* your charter. Against a nation with its own governance, its own land base, its own legitimacy claim, and treaty obligations owed by a Crown that no longer exists, that weapon has nothing to grip. §VII's *empire by invitation* and §IX's *charter rivals rather than fight them* lose their mechanism at the same moment, and §IX's standing failure mode inverts: here is a periphery that genuinely **is** a polity, and the Academy's instinct will be to treat it as a valley. In a fishery economy this is also the central external negotiation. It is a hole in §VI through §IX, not a missing paragraph.
4. **Is the founding vote's dissenter a named character?** The type requires someone who voted against and paid anyway. Whether that is one of the manuscript's existing women is a live and consequential choice.
