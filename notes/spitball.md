## Decided

- **Diana keeps her name.** Callisto was weighed and set aside *for now*. Recorded so it is not re-litigated from scratch: the case **for** was that Callisto is Diana's expelled companion rather than the goddess, which makes the name describe her position instead of her hobby, and that Juno's curse leaves Ursa Major circumpolar — always in the sky, never permitted to set — which is exactly a woman outside the perimeter. The case **against** is decisive on craft grounds: her shout-name would be *Callie* against Coelia's *Collie*, one vowel apart, in shared scenes. Also, the name pre-announces a specific fate.
- **Lewis's father is alive.** See *To fix*.
- **Collie becomes, by the book, a Patronus.** The informal favour economy hardens into an office with a title. "By the book" is already the manuscript's own register for the formal rotation (ch2 ¶49).
- **The marriage plot.** Somewhere around Lewis's college years, Collie introduces a girl from inside the university — marrying another protégé being the better bet than a huntress from the sticks, if the university is for the long haul.
- **The flight-risk mechanism.** The schoolteacher's beau stays behind instead of riding back, establishing the precedent Collie has to navigate later, when Lewis is doing his EE degree and gets sent out on the circuit.
- **Intimacy stays above the collarbone.** No explicit depiction. Fade to black, or narration that stays on the face and the arms.

  This is a description of ch2's existing method rather than a new constraint. The lodge scene is the most intimate in the book and is entirely non-sexual on the page: soup cooling, a hinge that will want greasing, a brush taken to grime, a stump massaged while they argue about other women. The charge is carried by labour and by touch, which is why it lands.

  Two consequences. The AO3 rating of **Mature** is comfortable and does not want raising. And the contraception and copper-IUD research is worldbuilding about *consequence and policy* — who is born, who is carded, what the Senate finds cheap — not groundwork for a scene the book will not write.

### The next chapters

**Ch3 — the water emergency.** The university detects a loss of pressure on the water mains and cuts the municipal connection in an emergency, to hold overpressure in its own network. The Elbakyan farm is on the far side of that boundary, and the works department runs desperate temporary piping to keep the next crop coming.

**This is a works-department chapter, not a Collie chapter.** Water trucks, damage control, and restoring supply to the horticultural spaces — plural, not only the medicinal patch the Elbakyans are responsible for. The scale is people doing physical work against a decision taken above them, which is ch2's mode. Collie in a meeting is a later chapter.

**And it is the second time the frats are mobilized** — hauling pipe and priming joints for the welding specialists. This is the load-bearing addition, because **the labor is the absorption mechanism**, and ch2 already planted it: the boys raise a barn "or whatnot" (ch2 ¶53), Bréal unlocks the toolbox and hands out the pliers, and the copper run is a work detail with a rifle on overwatch. A frat has already been shown operating as a labor company. Mobilization is the second formalization of a capability the reader has watched work.

**Open: what was the first mobilization?** "Second time" implies a precedent the reader should feel, and the arc depends on its character. If the first was defensive — perimeter, fire, the collapse itself — then the second being *labor* is the university deliberately teaching them a new function. Muscle repurposed as public works is a much better second beat than muscle used twice.

Technical grounding is in [`research/water-systems-under-failure.md`](../research/water-systems-under-failure.md). The four findings that bear hardest on the plot:

- **Trucks cannot irrigate.** An actively growing crop wants roughly 50 tonnes of water per hectare per day, which is five to ten full tanker loads per hectare per day. The same fleet that comfortably keeps people drinking cannot keep a field alive. The outcome is forced by arithmetic: greenhouses and perennial stock survive, open field crops do not.
- **Triage picks the Elbakyan patch on merit.** Horticultural triage under scarcity saves by *replaceability* — perennial and mother stock first, then seed crops, then established transplants, then field annuals last. Mature medicinal perennials sit at the top on their own. So the correct work order and a corrupt one are indistinguishable from the outcome, and no favouritism is required.
- **The emergency isolation is itself dangerous.** Closing a valve fast water-hammers the network and can burst it somewhere nobody is watching, so the cutoff has to be done slowly, by hand, while the pressure being raced is already falling. Valves that have not been exercised in years may also simply refuse to turn.
- **The real threat is cross-connection, not drought.** Tying a non-potable source into potable piping to move irrigation water is the mechanism of the 1933 Chicago outbreak, performed deliberately. The old world ended on water quality; the new one could end the same way, done by people trying to save a crop. Danger comes from the works department's own improvisation, which is a better engine than scarcity.

**Ch4 — Maddy.** Stuck in high school, tailing the MDs, and set up by Collie to become a proper doctor ever since Collie saw her take to the apothecary.

She is the only one of the three children never on the page as herself — so far an object in both chapters, a bike found at a yard sale and a cream Lewis carries out to the lodge. It is also the same story as Lewis's told from inside: he was steered toward EE by Maia (ch2 ¶1) and wants what he was pointed at. **Whether Maddy wants it is the question Lewis's chapter never had to ask.** If she does not, the covenant starts costing something from the inside, before Collie ever gets to Diana.

Her register for the title is pharmacy — *Compounding*, *Titration*, *Contraindications*. The `research/sources/who-essential-medicines-list-2023` material already in the repo points at her.

One connection to watch: if Maddy is tailing the MDs, she and Egghead share a small medical world, and Egghead is posted out to the town where Diana wants her leg looked at. Maddy is the link between Diana's leg and the person who could treat it, and she is also the one whose cream Lewis carries out there. She may already be an accessory without knowing to what.

**Ch5 — Collie and a professor of history.** A conversation months before a Senate meeting, on how their studies have taught them to least destructively absorb a dozen or so groups of organized and potentially violent young men.

Follows from ch3: once the frats have been mobilized twice, they are an instrument, and somebody has to decide what the instrument is. Two humanities academics working out the security architecture of a polity because they are the only people who have read about it happening before.

Research is in [`research/absorbing-armed-bands.md`](../research/absorbing-armed-bands.md), primed for grafting — its Section X lists the joints left open, of which **fraternities and student societies specifically, 19th–21st century** is the nearest analog and the most obvious next graft. What bears hardest on the chapter:

- **The precedent is a university, and the university won.** Medieval student *nations* were exactly this — coherent, armed, region-based sworn associations. Universities absorbed them by granting **constitutional standing** (*privilegium fori*: scholars answerable to university courts, not the town's). After Paris 1229 the students struck for two years and came back with a papal charter. After Oxford's St Scholastica Day riot in 1355 the university gained *more* power over the town, and the townspeople performed an annual penance for some 470 years. **Absorption works and the bill goes to whoever is outside the charter** — and this university has a town on the far side of a valve.
- **A dozen is the safety feature.** Plurality is doing more work than any charter provision: a dozen rivals cannot act in concert, present one negotiator, or strike together. So Collie's real interest is not suppressing violence but **preventing a merger** — which means the dangerous proposal at the Senate is the *reasonable* one. Consolidation is always argued on coordination, standardization, fairness in allocating work, a single point of contact, and every one of those arguments is correct. The Praetorian Guard was a set of cohorts until Sejanus put them in one barracks for convenience; then they made and unmade emperors for three centuries.
- **The near-term risk has a name: Francesco Sforza**, contracted as Milan's condottiere, who took Milan in 1450. Bréal is a well-liked captain of a labor company who notices everything and declines to escalate. He is precisely who this happens to.
- **The long-term risk is generational, not moral.** Janissaries were built unable to marry or own property *specifically* so they could not become a dynasty. Every constraint eroded by ordinary succession — successors inherited the capability without inheriting why the constraints existed — until the corps had to be destroyed outright in 1826. That is the same shape as Collie's patronage: arrangements that need continuous enforcement are spent slowly by nobody's decision.
- **The cost of the labor route is precedent.** After the first mobilization there is a capability, after the second an expectation, and eventually the university cannot do infrastructure without them — a veto they never had to seize. Necessity is leverage and it accrues unbidden.
- **The benign model is the Civilian Conservation Corps**, which absorbed three million young men into forestry and infrastructure under Army camp administration — and which arrived directly after the Bonus Army marched on Washington, with Roosevelt opening enrollment to the second march's veterans. An organized body that had marched on the capital was absorbed by being offered work. That it is remembered as conservation policy rather than as the management of dangerous men is the whole point.

**The reversal this collects on:** ch1 ¶9 has the university making Collie *teach freshmen about colonialism without mentioning Said and economics without mentioning Friedman* — it censored its own humanities as ornament. Now it needs a classicist and a historian to work out how a polity survives its own armed young men. The department treated as decoration turns out to be the strategic studies faculty. Also: the governance body is a **Senate**, and a classicist advising a Senate about chartering armed bands is a pun that does not need pointing at.

**A Diana chapter — unnumbered, but before the marriage plot.** Her quotidian. Her own traps and her unease about them since one took her foot. More small game. Something with her mother. Something about jam, which leads at the end to looking forward to Lewis's next visit.

**The constraint that governs it: Lewis does not define her.** She has her own life and her own town, which she goes to **every other other week** — alternating with his visits (ch2 ¶5 has the frat coming every other weekend). That interleaves her life with his *absence* rather than organising it around his presence, and it means she has a whole week he has never seen. Structural rather than asserted, which is why it works.

Research is in [`research/trapping-and-small-game.md`](../research/trapping-and-small-game.md).

- **Make the trap the injury and the lack of care the loss.** A crushed foot goes to infection and then to a below-knee amputation, weeks apart — which is consistent with ch2 ¶121's metal leg and stump. It also makes her amputation the same story as Nona at the clinic (ch1 ¶43–59), and turns ch2 ¶129 — *can that Egghead help with the leg* — into the second act of the injury rather than idle curiosity. **The university's card is on her body.**
- **Her unease has an administrative shape.** Sets are not marked, because marking makes them findable, so the line lives in memory and is refreshed by walking it. The dread is *"I have to remember all of them, and once I didn't."* It also supplies the chapter's clock: the circuit has to be walked today, because a held animal suffers, a dead one spoils, and a caught one is bait for something else.
- **The jam is survival, not domesticity, and ch2 ¶103 already says so.** *"Eating nothing but rabbits is how you starve"* is rabbit starvation — lean meat has a hard protein ceiling and hare is a few percent fat, so a person can eat continuously and die. The missing pieces are fat and carbohydrate. Her jam and her rendered fat are the two halves of what makes the meat survivable, and the honey-for-jam swap in ch2 ¶137 is two women trading calories they each need. Nona's line was a warning; Diana lives inside it. **Pemmican — dried lean meat, rendered fat, dried berries — is all three elements of this chapter in one keeping ration.**
- **She is not unaffiliated everywhere, only with the university.** If she trades in the town she has standing in an economy that owes the university nothing, and BC registers traplines as inheritable exclusive tenure — so a recognised line is a form of *title*. The card is not the only card, which matters for the marriage plot: she is choosing between two societies, not between Lewis and nothing.
- **Two guards.** The Lewis beat must be last and small, one line, the way ch2 lands on the apiary — otherwise the chapter is about him regardless. And the unease has to cost her something on the page: a hesitation that loses an animal, a set laid badly because she is flinching, a place she will not walk any more.

Her register for the title is trapping: *Trapline*, *Sprung*, *Runs*, *The Check*, and **_Blind Set_** — the technical term for a trap on a trail with no bait at all, relying entirely on knowing where something will walk.

**Why this chapter has to exist:** Diana appears in exactly one chapter. That is thin ground on which to break a reader's heart, and the marriage plot will read as disposal rather than tragedy until she has more page time. Related craft note recorded during the same conversation: **the university girl is currently a device, not a person** — "slide-rule jockey" was the tell. Give her something Diana genuinely cannot give Lewis that he actually wants, and make her a steered protégé with no more say than he has, and there is no rival to root against and nobody to blame. Two people who like each other, used by the same machine, is much worse to read and much better to have written.

## Readings

Observations about what is already on the page. These are interpretations, not intentions — weigh them as such.

### The water is the book's loaded gun

Ch1 ¶11 has the Biology department answering a government RFP for regional water quality assurance. Ch1 ¶13: *"The water had a marginally higher chlorine content two years after, and Collie did not notice. The next contract review proudly presented downward pressure on the UV disinfection budget."* That is the collapse mechanism, delivered flat, and nobody in the book knows it.

The weak payoff is a revelation scene. The strong one is that **the same budget logic runs again** — the university is the state now, it supplies water to the region, and it holds contract reviews. Collie has moved from the person who did not notice to the person in the room.

### Madam Swartz's silence is an offer

Ch2: she says nothing, serves the jam, bakes it into a cake, sends a slice to Bréal (¶143). Against ch1, where she got an unaffiliated woman treated by threatening a clinic with its own optics (ch1 ¶43, ch1 ¶59) — she is the only person in the book who knows how to buy a stranger into the system, and she has done it before, for Nona.

Diana is unaffiliated, has no surname the university would recognise, and wants to know whether a doctor can look at her leg (ch2 ¶129). **Lewis cannot solve that. Collie can.** The jam reads as *I know, and I am waiting to be asked* — which makes Lewis's arc learning to ask, and the cost Collie spending standing in an economy where standing is the currency.

### The rounds are the crime, not the copper

Copper is theft the whole crew commits together; Bréal hands out the pliers. The second pile in ch2 ¶57 — rounds sorted into Lewis's own backpack — is different in kind. If Diana is outside the perimeter with a rifle, those are hers, and Lewis is arming someone the university does not recognise.

### The three children became three technical bases

Ch1 ¶23: *"Lewis never did find a proper three-player game, the teams never were balanced."* Now Lewis is electrical, Maddy makes the ointments, Nadia grows the ginger. Three things a city-state cannot run without. If deliberate, that line is a thesis and the book has a natural three-POV spine.

### Roman patronage is already in chapter one

Worth knowing before the office gets formalised, because two objects are already doing this work:

- **The laminated cards** (ch1 ¶75) are a ***tessera***. Rome had the object twice: the *tessera hospitalis*, a token split between households proving a hereditary protection tie, and the *tessera frumentaria*, which entitled the bearer to the grain dole — and ch1 ¶9 has Collie filmed for a video *about the grain dole and what a government owes its people*. The token and the dole are in the same chapter. **Diana has no card. That is why she cannot be treated.**
- **The jam and the mead and the honey** are a ***sportula*** — the "little basket," the dole a patron handed clients at the door, food under the Republic, cash by Juvenal. Chapter two's economy of jars moving between households is clientela in operation.

Roman patronage was extra-legal, deniable, and enforced by reputation rather than statute — held together by *fides*. That is exactly Collie's method in ch1: no standing to compel anyone, only a phone in her pocket and the threat of bad optics.

The constraint that cuts in her favour, from the Twelve Tables: ***patronus si clienti fraudem fecerit, sacer esto*** — a patron who defrauds his client is accursed, outside the law's protection. **The obligation runs downhill first.** A Patronus who abandons a client is the one who is damned, and Collie would know it, because she taught it.

Vocabulary if the office is formalised: *fides* (binding good faith), *beneficium* (the favour given) against *officium* (the duty owed back), *gratia* (accrued standing — the actual currency), *clientela* (the institution), *salutatio* (morning attendance at the patron's door), *patrona* (correct and attested — women held patronage over freedpersons and over whole communities). And ***nomenclator***, the attendant whose entire job was remembering which client was which: a university that must adjudicate affiliation needs one, and the office is sitting unfilled.

### The tragedy is already structural

Collie improvises patronage in ch1 to save one unaffiliated woman. If it becomes an office, it becomes a category, and **categories exclude.** The instrument she invented to get Nona through a clinic door is the instrument that will be cited to keep Diana outside it. She does not have to turn; the institution only has to succeed.

### Craft notes on the two new threads

**The marriage plot** works *provided Collie is right*, and she is — the university is the long haul, and the book has twice shown that being outside the walls means going untreated. Keep her right and it is tragedy; let her be petty and it collapses into arranged-marriage-versus-true-love. Two ways out of that groove: make the university girl a protégé too, with no more say than Lewis, so it is two people bred for institutional continuity rather than a rival; or have Collie offer to card Diana — as the price, or worse, as a genuine kindness — since the moment Diana is affiliated she is inside the clientela and therefore under Collie. Accepting protection for her is also handing her over.

**The beau** needs a function or his staying costs the university nothing and there is no problem for Collie to navigate. Terrace's line solves this: *carpenter*. The cheapest way to plant it earlier is ch2 ¶41, where two replacement guards are being briefed — make him one, and his staying means the town holds a guard the university is not counting, which is the nomenclator problem rather than a romance.

The consequence to reach for is **deletion, not punishment.** No sanction, no pursuit: he falls off the roll and the coverage goes with him, and then something ordinary and medical happens and there is no card. Anger can be weathered; deletion cannot. It also keeps Collie sympathetic while making her frightening — she is not the one who punishes him, she is the one who has to write the memo about next time, and that memo is what traps Lewis.

**Keep it off the page.** The book's method is that large things happen in subordinate clauses — the chlorine, the UV budget, *"the trucks will be going elsewhere next month."* The reader should never watch the beau decide. A convoy comes back, somebody counts, the count is short. Staged as a scene it becomes a subplot and will outweigh Lewis's version when his turn comes.

### The sisters line holds

Ch2 ¶127, *"Well, they are my sisters"* — the only place in either chapter where the covenant kinship is asserted, and it arrives inside a deflection during an argument about something else, which is the strongest possible placement. It is also what makes the marriage plot necessary: if Maddy and Nadia are his sisters, the household cannot solve its continuity problem internally, and Collie has to import someone.

Two things to watch. It is a **single point of failure** — the kind of subordinate clause a tightening pass removes, taking the covenant thesis out of the manuscript with it; it may eventually want one corroborating instance in a different register. And Bréal's *"lil bro"* (ch2 ¶61) is the same vocabulary in a frat-casual key twenty lines earlier; the two are far enough apart in tone to be fine, but if fictive-kin address spreads further, "sisters" starts to mean less.

Diana calls Nona *"that woman"* in the exchange immediately following (ch2 ¶105). Against Lewis's *auntie*, she is outside the kin vocabulary and does not use it. If deliberate, one of the sharpest small things in the chapter.
