## Why 7–9 sit where they do

*Renumbered 2026-08-11 when **the minutes chapter was cut** — Terrace: "leave the frat thread as finished at the ending of Collie and the Historian." Renumbered again 2026-08-12 when ***Slush*** was inserted at 3. Reasoning and the surviving devices are in [`spitball.md`](spitball.md).*

- **Slush at 3 solved the Diana problem better than the cut did, and by addition rather than subtraction.** Inserting it pushes Diana's chapter from 6 to 7 — *further* from ch2, which is the thing the minutes cut was meant to fix. It does not matter, because **Diana is now on the page in ch3**, seen clinically by a stranger, so she is no longer five chapters from her only appearance. She has two before her own.
- **3 previews the outside before the machine speaks.** Four, five and six are all the institution talking — a water emergency, a ward, and a policy decided over mead. **Slush shows the reader what has no recourse *first***, so the machine chapters are read against it instead of establishing the norm.
- **7 breaks the run and returns to what 3 opened.** Diana is the person the institution is going to crush; the tonal contrast is the point, and the rhyme with Slush is the reason the run can be three chapters long without going inert.
- **8 before 9 is deliberate.** Maia's chapter shows a marriage functioning as a partnership of labour — he builds the clock, she determines its rate, neither half works alone. Putting that immediately before Collie engineers a marriage makes the engineering visible without a word of comment.
- **9 is last** because it needs Lewis in college, which is a time skip, and because it cannot land until Diana has had a chapter.
- **The obstetric chapter is contemporary with 9** — Maddy is 18, so Lewis is college-aged in the same season. Sitting them adjacent gives two kinship transactions in one year, one accidental and one engineered, with no comment required.
